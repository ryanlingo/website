## Background

My academic training is in philosophy. I was most interested in philosophy of mind, philosophy of action, decision theory, and practical reason, which together ask how people think, decide, and act, and what practical wisdom looks like. Philosophy is where I learned to think critically, read closely, and write clearly. It taught me to slow down, find the assumptions under an argument, and say exactly what I mean.

I was also a professional poker player. I started online, and after [Black Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Friday_(poker)) in 2011, when the US government shut down the largest online poker sites, I played live only. Poker put the decision theory I studied into practice, because each hand is a choice made with incomplete information, and you learn to judge a decision by how good it was and not by how it turned out. I've always loved games and puzzles, and poker was the hardest one I've played.

I left academic philosophy for a job as a front end developer, and I taught myself the work. After the company I worked for was acquired, I moved from development into data, where I worked on analytics and designed dashboards, and I taught myself Python along the way. Then I taught at a coding bootcamp for a while. After that, I went out on my own and did freelance consulting on software and data projects.

Now I'm a developer advocate and applied AI research engineer at [99P Labs](https://www.99plabs.com/), the Ohio office of [Honda Research Institute](https://usa.honda-ri.com/). I work with student teams at many universities, and you can see some of their projects in the [Student Innovation Showcase](https://www.99plabs.com/studentinnovationshowcase). My research focuses on new work in AI. My papers are on the [[research/index|Research]] page, and my talks are on the [[speaking|Speaking]] page.

Looking back, the common thread is that I'm a problem solver, and each field gave me a different kind of tool for solving problems:

- **Philosophy** gave me ideas, i.e. ways to define a problem clearly and reason about it.
- **Software** gave me a way to build things that solve the problem.
- **Data** gave me a way to see what is actually happening, and to check whether a solution works.
- **AI** gives me a way to build tools that work alongside people, and it draws on the other three.

That's also how I think about technology. Inspired by John Dewey, I see technology as the tools people invent and use to resolve problems they've run into, and ideas count as tools as much as a hammer does.
